Pacing Source on GitHub

Find the beat you're missing.

Pacing reads the minute-by-minute timeline of your League of Legends games — not the scoreboard — and tells you where the lead slips away, what your build missed, and which games to go and watch.

Get it on GitHub

The idea

▲ Example finding

You are near the pit for only 27% of rift heralds

Across 45 heralds taken by either team, you were within range for 12. Your team secured 92% of the ones you were close to, and 30% of the ones you were not.

That is the kind of thing a scoreboard cannot tell you, and a KDA never will. Every finding carries an estimated cost in gold per game and links to the exact matches it came from.

What it measures

Where the lead slips

Gold, CS and experience against the enemy player in your own role, every minute of every game. The five-minute window where you lose the most ground is marked on the chart.

Where you actually go

Your routes drawn on the map from real position data, a death heatmap, and whether you were close enough to help when each objective fell.

Builds that answer the game

Whether you bought resistances and Grievous Wounds in the games that called for them — judged on the damage the enemy actually dealt and the damage you actually took, never guessed from champion names.

One thing at a time

Pick a single metric to work on. Pacing remembers where you started and tells you whether it moved, instead of handing you the same list of six problems every week.

How it works

  1. Enter your Riot ID. Pacing pulls your recent ranked games and their full match timelines from the Riot Games API.
  2. It compares you to the player in your role — the enemy jungler, or your lane opponent — minute by minute, rather than to a league average.
  3. You get a ranked list of problems, each with a size in gold and a link to the games it came from.

Built to be honest

Nothing true by definition

"You die more in games you lose" is true for every player alive and cannot be acted on. Every finding is measured against your own opponent, or against another phase of your own game.

Corrected for survivorship

Losses run longer than wins, so late-game averages drift for reasons that have nothing to do with how you played. The curves stop once fewer than 60% of games are still running.

Runs on your machine

Pacing is a local app. Your API key stays in a file on your computer, downloaded games are cached locally, and nothing is uploaded, shared or sold.

Readable without colour

Wins are blue and losses orange rather than green and red, because roughly 1 in 12 men cannot reliably separate those. Every value is written out in text too.

Open source — github.com/hjalmerhertz/pacing. Built with Next.js and TypeScript.
